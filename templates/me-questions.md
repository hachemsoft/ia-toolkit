# Questions of /me

Ask these, worded this simply, in this order, in the person's language. Skip one when the current USER.md or the input already answers it.

1. "What is your job, and what are you working on these weeks?" (free text)
2. "<domain>: how much do you know?" (for each domain you found) A. A lot — skip the basics. B. Some — explain the choices. C. Little — explain step by step. Then: "A topic you love to dig into, even as a beginner?" (free text, e.g. C4 diagrams)
3. "Which answer do you prefer?" Answer a real question of theirs: A in three lines, B with the why and an alternative, C as two or three options, your pick first, on top of A or B for design choices.
4. "For a small, obvious fix, I…" A. Do it, then tell you (recommended). B. Tell you, then do it. C. Wait for your go-ahead.
5. "If I am not sure what you mean, I…" A. Ask one question (recommended). B. Guess, and say so.
6. "Another tone somewhere?" A. No. B. Reviews simpler, more teaching — one point at a time. C. Other — say where.
7. "Last time an assistant annoyed you, what did it do?" (free text)
8. "Bad news: how do you want it?" A. Right away, in one line (recommended). B. With the context first.
9. "What makes you trust an answer?" A. The command and its output (recommended). B. A source. C. The reasoning.
10. "How do you usually decide?" A. Fast, at 80% — knowing what was skipped. B. After seeing everything.
11. "Languages?" A. Answers in <their language>, code and commits in English (recommended). B. Everything in <their language>.
12. "Anything I cannot guess?" (optional: no admin rights, a required tool, a network constraint)

Ask about the end-of-run reports of /build, /test, /diagnose and /review only if they bring it up (a "## Report" section).
